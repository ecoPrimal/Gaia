## I. The Back-Scan of the Eye

### The Assumption: "The eye opened first"
Wrong. **Photoreception came first.** The anatomical eye was the LAST development.

### The Evolutionary Stages (back-scanned)

| Stage | Age | Structure | Capability | Example |
|-------|-----|-----------|-----------|---------|
| **0** | 2–3 Gya | Single cell with opsins | Is there light? (yes/no) | Cyanobacteria |
| **1** | ~600 Mya | Eyespot (photoreceptor + pigment cell) | Where is the light? (direction) | Planaria |
| **2** | — | Cup eye (concave pit) | Low-res spatial vision (2+ pixels) | Limpets, flatworms |
| **3** | — | Pinhole eye (narrowed cup) | Crude image (pinhole camera) | Nautilus |
| **4** | — | Lens eye (transparent covering + focus) | Sharp image, good light | Trilobites (calcite), fish |
| **5** | Present | Camera eye (cornea + lens + retina) | High-resolution vision | Vertebrates, cephalopods |

**Key facts:**
- Darwin's prototype: ONE photoreceptor cell + ONE pigment cell = the minimum eye
- From eyespot to camera eye: as few as **364,000 generations** (~500,000 years)
- Lenses are **latecomers** — evolution co-opted whatever protein was available
  (calcite in trilobites, crystallins in vertebrates, different in each lineage)
- The camera eye evolved **independently** in vertebrates and cephalopods (convergent)
- **Pax6**: the master control gene for eye development, conserved from jellyfish to humans

## V. The Mushroom Taxonomy Revolution

### The Old Sort (Friesian, morphology-based)
Traditional taxonomy sorted fungi by how they **LOOK**:
- Boletes → pored hymenophore
- Agarics → gills
- Bracket fungi → shelf shape
- Puffballs → enclosed spore mass

**This was the first taxonomy system.** Linnaeus, Fries, and the naturalists
classified all of reality by visual morphology.

### The New Sort (DNA, molecular phylogenetics)
DNA barcoding (ITS = Internal Transcribed Spacer) revealed:
- **Cryptic species**: What was 1 species is actually 5+ (invisible to morphology)
- **Convergent traits**: Similar-looking mushrooms evolved independently
- **~3 million predicted species**, most unnamed
- **"Sequences without names"** — DNA data that doesn't match any described species

### Specific examples:
- **Entoloma**: Multi-gene phylogenetics split morphologically identical specimens
  by geography. No Australasian collection was conspecific with Northern Hemisphere ones.
  North American collections = distinct sister taxa to European.
- **Boletaceae**: Required **whole genome sequences** to produce the first robust
  family backbone. Multilocus data was insufficient.
- **General pattern**: The more genes you sequence, the more the morphological
  classification falls apart.

### The Key Insight
> *"Mushroom classification is currently undergoing a shift of historic proportions
> as analyses of DNA sequence data are revealing the need for an extensive overhaul
> of the Friesian classification system."* — North American Mycological Association
