## III. ANTI-SLAPP CASE LAW

> *"A person shall not be subjected to civil liability for communicating a statement of a matter of public concern..."*

- **Thomas M. Cooley Law School v. Doe 1**, 833 NW2d 331 (Mich. App. 2013) — Anti-SLAPP protections apply when statements relate to matters of public concern, even if the speaker's motives are mixed.

- **Lott v. Lech**, Michigan Court of Appeals, No. 365726 (2023) — Affirmed that filing government complaints and publishing truthful public records constitutes protected activity under anti-SLAPP.

- **NAACP v. Claiborne Hardware Co.**, 458 U.S. 886 (1982) — The First Amendment protects vigorous advocacy, including boycotts and public campaigns, aimed at achieving political, social, and economic change.

- **New York Times Co. v. Sullivan**, 376 U.S. 254 (1964) — Public figures must show actual malice. He is unquestionably a public figure.

- **Snyder v. Phelps**, 562 U.S. 443 (2011) — Speech on matters of public concern receives heightened First Amendment protection, even when offensive to the subject.

My conduct — documenting public records, contacting the press, filing regulatory complaints, and advocating for parental access — falls squarely within these protections.
